Dual-Axis Solar Tracker

Mechatronics & Embedded Controls

Type

Independent Project

Focus

Closed-Loop Controls

Timeline

2026

Key Tools

Onshape, Linear Actuators, Sensors

Project Overview

To maximize the efficiency of photovoltaic systems, I designed and built a dual-axis solar tracker capable of autonomously following a light source. This mechatronics project required a complete integration of mechanical design, sensor calibration, and embedded control programming.

Mechanical Design & Torque Requirements

The mechanical structure was modeled entirely in Onshape, focusing on stability and smooth actuation across two degrees of freedom (pan and tilt). Before fabrication, I calculated the dynamic loads and torque requirements to ensure the selected servo motors could comfortably rotate the panel assembly without stalling or experiencing excessive jitter.

Closed-Loop Control Architecture

The system is driven by an ESP32 microcontroller that actively reads analog inputs from an array of ambient light sensors. I developed a closed-loop control algorithm that processes these differential light values, calculates the error gradient, and outputs synchronized signals to the servo drivers. This continuous feedback loop dynamically adjusts the panel to remain perfectly perpendicular to the strongest light source.
